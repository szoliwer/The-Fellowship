**User:** I'm building fin-ray style grippers for picking strawberries and raspberries. The basic design is two flexible TPU fingers with internal ribs that wrap around the fruit when pushed. In bench tests with store-bought berries, we still get bruising on maybe 15 percent of raspberries, usually visible a day later. I think the issue is pressure concentration at the rib contact points. How would you approach reducing peak contact pressure without losing grip?

**Assistant:** Pressure concentration at the rib contacts is a likely culprit. Some approaches: angle the ribs so that they bend more uniformly and distribute load across the finger surface; reduce rib stiffness near the tip where raspberries are most fragile; and add a soft skin, such as a thin silicone layer or foam, over the contact surface. Pressure-sensitive film placed between finger and a fruit-shaped dummy can show where peaks occur. Also consider controlling the approach speed, since impact loading on contact can bruise as much as steady grip force.

**User:** Good ideas. We tried a 2mm silicone skin last week and it cut visible bruising a lot, maybe down to 6 percent, but now the fingers slip more on wet berries. I'm also suspicious of our test setup. Store-bought berries are already a few days old and refrigerated, so they don't behave like freshly picked fruit. I really need to test on an actual farm or packing line with fresh fruit, but we don't have that connection.

**Assistant:** The slip problem could be addressed by texturing the silicone skin, such as micro-ridges or a pattern inspired by finger pads, which can improve wet friction without much extra stiffness. Your concern about the test fruit is well founded; firmness and skin strength change considerably after harvest and refrigeration, so bench results may underestimate or misrepresent bruising rates. Partnering with a grower or packing facility would also expose you to real conditions like variable ripeness, dust, and cycle-time pressure. Agricultural extension programs or produce cooperatives are sometimes good starting points for finding a willing site.
