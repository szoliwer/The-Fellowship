**User:** Our lab wants to release a gripper design as open hardware, and someone suggested using the CERN Open Hardware Licence. I'm skeptical about how much a licence like that actually protects. With software, copyright clearly covers code. But for a physical object, isn't most of the design not copyrightable? If a company builds and sells our design without following the licence, could anyone do anything about it?

**Assistant:** Your skepticism is reasonable. Hardware licences mostly rely on copyright in the design documents, such as CAD files, drawings, and documentation. Copying and modifying those files is covered, but manufacturing a physical object from the design is often not a copyright act, especially for functional parts. Patents protect functional inventions, but open hardware projects rarely file them. So someone could study your published drawings and build the device without being bound by the licence. The CERN licences try to strengthen this through contract-like terms, but enforceability varies by jurisdiction.

**User:** So the licence is more of a social norm than a legal shield. That's fine honestly. Our goal is adoption, not exclusivity. But I'd like modifications to be shared back, especially for farm settings where people will adapt things. Is the strongly reciprocal variant worth it, or does it just scare off companies that might help commercialise it?

**Assistant:** That framing is right. Licences shape expectations and community behaviour more than they guarantee enforcement. The strongly reciprocal variant requires anyone distributing modified designs to share their changes under the same terms. It signals your intent clearly, and many contributors respect it. But some companies avoid reciprocal licences because they want to keep improvements proprietary. The weakly reciprocal option allows combining with proprietary parts while keeping your core design open. If commercial uptake matters, weak reciprocity is often a reasonable compromise, and you can pair it with a clear contribution guide to encourage sharing.
