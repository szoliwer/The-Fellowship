**User:** We've reached the point where the berry gripper needs real field trials. In the lab we've got bruising down to about 4 percent on raspberries with the textured silicone fin-ray, but everything is with store-bought fruit and a static rig. I want to find a grower or packing house willing to let us test on a real line for a few weeks. What should I prepare before approaching them, and what would make it worth their while?

**Assistant:** Prepare a short, clear proposal: what you want to test, how long, how much space and staff time it requires, and how you will ensure the trial does not disrupt their operations or damage marketable fruit. Bring data from the lab and a demonstration video. Offer something valuable, such as bruising and throughput comparisons with their current process, early access to the technology, or help with a specific bottleneck they face. Address liability and food safety, including food-grade materials and cleaning procedures. Being flexible about timing around peak harvest helps too.

**User:** Food safety is something I hadn't thought enough about. Our TPU is not certified food-contact, and the silicone skin is a hobby-grade material. I should switch to food-grade versions before any trial. I'm also thinking a packing line might be easier than in-field picking, since the environment is more controlled and we could mount the gripper on a fixed station. But field picking is where the real labour shortage is.

**Assistant:** Switching to food-grade materials is important, and it will also make conversations with growers easier. Since material changes affect stiffness and friction, rerun your bench tests with the new materials first. Starting on a packing line is a sensible step: conditions are more controlled, you can collect many trials quickly, and integration is simpler. Data from there can de-risk a later field trial. You could frame it as a phased partnership, with the packing line first and field picking later, which may appeal to growers who want to see results before committing more resources.
