**User:** Our hydrophones keep getting covered in algae and barnacles over a deployment. On one site the recording level dropped noticeably after month three and I'm fairly sure it's fouling on the element. We've tried copper tape and a little mesh guard. Has anyone found a better solution, or a way to correct for the drift afterwards? Retrieving them more often is not really an option given boat time.

**Assistant:** Fouling is one of the most common problems on long deployments. Options include antifouling coatings designed for transducers (silicone foul-release coatings are popular because they don't affect acoustics much), copper mesh cages at a small distance from the element rather than directly on it, and orienting the hydrophone downward to reduce settlement. For correction, some teams play a calibration tone at deployment and recovery, or track a stable reference like the high-frequency shrimp noise floor over time to estimate sensitivity drift and correct it.

**User:** Using the shrimp floor as an internal reference is clever, though shrimp activity changes seasonally too, so I'd need to be careful. I like the idea of a calibration tone at both ends. We've now got four years of these deployments and I keep a log of fouling state with photos at each recovery, so I could probably model drift against how bad the fouling looked.

**Assistant:** That fouling log is valuable. You could score fouling from the photos on a simple ordinal scale, then fit sensitivity change against fouling score and deployment duration, using calibration tones where you have them as anchors. Even an approximate correction model, documented with uncertainty, would make the four-year archive far more usable for long-term trend analysis. It would also help others who use similar units in similar waters.
