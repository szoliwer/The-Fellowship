**User:** Quick sanity check. Our lab and a lot of reef papers use the acoustic complexity index as a proxy for biodiversity and reef health. But when I plot ACI against our diver fish counts at the same sites, the relationship is weak, and in some months ACI goes up when fish counts go down. I'm starting to doubt whether ACI measures fish at all or just something like shrimp activity or boat noise. Am I missing something?

**Assistant:** You're not alone in that doubt. ACI measures how much intensity varies between adjacent time frames within frequency bins, so it responds to any temporally variable sound, including snapping shrimp, rain, waves and vessel passes, not only fish. Several comparative studies have found inconsistent correlations between ACI and visual census counts, with results depending on site, frequency band, and season. Restricting ACI to the low-frequency band where most fish calls sit (roughly 50 to 1,500 Hz) and removing boat-noise periods often improves things, but the index is still a summary, not a count.

**User:** We did compute it across the full band, so that could be part of it. Even so, I feel like the field has adopted these indices because they're easy, not because they've been validated. I don't want to build my whole recovery monitoring story on a number I don't trust. What would a more rigorous validation even look like?

**Assistant:** A rigorous validation would pair acoustic data with independent ground truth at matching spatial and temporal scales: repeated visual surveys or video at the hydrophone location, ideally across a range of fish densities. You'd test several indices in band-limited form, check whether they predict abundance or richness out of sample at new sites, and report where they fail. Comparing indices against detection counts of specific call types would also be informative, since call counts are closer to what fish are doing. Framing it as a validation study rather than a monitoring study could itself be a publishable contribution.
