**User:** I finally ran a proper comparison. Band-limited ACI, a bioacoustic index, and acoustic entropy against diver fish counts across eleven reefs and three years. Best correlation was around 0.3 and it flipped sign at two sites. I'm torn between writing this up as a negative result and trying yet another index. My gut says the indices just don't track abundance reliably. How would you present this?

**Assistant:** A correlation around 0.3 with sign reversals across sites is a meaningful finding, and a clearly presented negative result is useful to the field. I'd present site-level relationships alongside the pooled one, show where and when the indices diverge, and test candidate drivers of divergence like shrimp activity, vessel noise and hydrophone drift. Including a simple event-based measure such as dusk chorus intensity for comparison would show whether any acoustic metric tracks abundance better.

**User:** I like that framing, it's less 'indices are bad' and more 'here is when they fail and why'. I do think there's a methodological question that many people in reef acoustics are quietly sceptical about. I'd love to compare notes with others who have independent survey data, to see if what we see is general or specific to our reefs.

**Assistant:** That would strengthen the conclusions considerably. A multi-site, multi-region comparison using a shared protocol, with the same indices, the same bands and similar survey methods, would address whether the failure is general. You could propose a common analysis script and invite groups with paired acoustic and survey data to run it. Even a few additional regions would make the result much harder to dismiss as site-specific.
