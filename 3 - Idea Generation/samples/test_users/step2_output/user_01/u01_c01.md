**User:** I'm planning the next round of hydrophone deployments on our reef sites and trying to settle the duty cycle. Last time we recorded 1 minute every 10 minutes at 48 kHz and the batteries lasted about five months. I'd like to stretch that to a full year so we only service the units once per season window, but I'm worried that sparser sampling will miss the dusk chorus peaks. How would you think about the trade-off?

**Assistant:** A good way to frame it is by which temporal patterns you need to resolve. Dusk and dawn choruses are short but predictable, so you could use an adaptive schedule: denser sampling (say 1 in 5 minutes) for two hours around sunrise and sunset, and sparser sampling (1 in 20) the rest of the day. That often saves 30 to 40 percent of power while keeping the chorus structure intact. You could also drop the sample rate to 24 kHz if your target fish calls sit below 2 kHz, which halves storage. If you have last season's data, try subsampling it at different schedules and checking how much the daily chorus metrics change.

**User:** That's a nice idea, using our own old data to simulate the schedules. We do have snapping shrimp energy up to 20 kHz that I care about for the overall soundscape, so I'm reluctant to drop to 24 kHz. I think I'll keep 48 kHz and do the crepuscular-weighted schedule. We have eleven reefs now with units going back four years, so I can test it across sites rather than just one.

**Assistant:** Keeping 48 kHz makes sense if shrimp snaps are part of your soundscape picture. With four years across eleven reefs you can do a fairly robust test: pick a few representative months per site, subsample at each candidate schedule, and compare daily chorus timing, peak level and band energy against the full record. If the error is small and consistent across sites, you can justify the new schedule in your methods. Also log the exact schedule in each file's metadata so future analyses aren't confused by the change.
