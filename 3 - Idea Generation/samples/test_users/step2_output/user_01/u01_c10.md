**User:** We've decided to make our multi-year reef recordings openly available once the data paper is out. It's a lot of data, roughly 40 terabytes. I'm wondering about practical things: where to host it, what metadata standard to use, and whether to release everything raw or also provide some processed products like daily spectrogram summaries. I'd like other researchers, including people outside ecology, to actually be able to use it.

**Assistant:** For hosting, large research data repositories or institutional cloud storage with a persistent identifier work well; some offer free hosting for open environmental data. For metadata, use a recognised standard for acoustic monitoring that records location, depth, device, calibration, sample rate and schedule, and include deployment-level and file-level records. Releasing both raw audio and processed products is ideal: long-term spectral averages, hourly band levels and index values make the data approachable for people who won't download 40 terabytes.

**User:** Processed products are a good idea, especially for people outside ecology like ML researchers or oceanographers who might want ready-to-use features. I'll also include the fouling scores and calibration notes so nobody gets misled by sensitivity drift. It feels like this archive could be one of the most useful things our lab offers, more than any single paper.

**Assistant:** Long-term, multi-site archives with good documentation often end up being cited and reused more than the original analyses. Including fouling scores and calibration notes is exactly the kind of detail that builds trust. A short usage guide with example notebooks for loading data and computing simple features would lower the barrier further, and a clear license and citation request will help you track reuse.
