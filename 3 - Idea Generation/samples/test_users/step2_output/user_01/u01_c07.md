**User:** I'm writing a short description of our recording archive for a data paper. We have about four years of continuous duty-cycled recordings from eleven reefs, with matching temperature loggers, deployment logs, fouling photos and some diver surveys. I want to describe it so other groups know it exists and could use it. What should a data paper like this include?

**Assistant:** A data paper typically covers: site descriptions with coordinates and habitat context, equipment and calibration details, the recording schedule and any changes over time, file formats and naming conventions, quality-control steps (including known gaps, fouling periods and noise events), associated datasets like temperature and surveys, and access terms. A few example spectrograms and summary figures help readers see what's in it. If you want to broaden the ecological picture, you could also consider pairing future deployments with environmental DNA sampling at each site, which can give species lists that complement acoustic detections.

**User:** Thanks, that list is helpful. On the eDNA suggestion, that's not something we'll do. It's outside what our team does and we don't have the lab capacity or the budget for it, so I'd rather keep the archive focused on acoustics and the supporting environmental data we already collect. I'll make sure the deployment logs and the fouling notes are well documented though.

**Assistant:** Understood, keeping the scope focused on acoustics plus your existing environmental data is sensible and makes the data paper cleaner. Documenting the deployment logs and fouling notes thoroughly will add a lot of value, since those are exactly the details other users need to judge data quality. You could include a table listing every deployment with start and end dates, unit ID, depth, schedule, calibration status and a fouling score at recovery.
