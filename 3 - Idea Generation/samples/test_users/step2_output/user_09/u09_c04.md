**User:** I've been coding my citation dataset and the pattern is stark: in my pilot set, studies reporting a positive effect get roughly three times as many citations as null studies of similar size. I want to make sure I'm not fooling myself. What confounders should I worry about when claiming positive results are cited more than null results?

**Assistant:** Key confounders include journal prestige, since positive studies may land in higher-visibility venues; publication date, as older papers accumulate more citations; sample size and design quality; author prominence; and whether the study was the first on the topic. Self-citation can inflate counts too. You could also check whether null studies are cited in a different way, for example only in passing within lists. Adjusting for venue and year in your model, plus a sensitivity analysis excluding self-citations, would address the most obvious concerns.

**User:** Venue is a big one, I'll add it. Here's a side question. I ran my image duplication detector over the same set of papers out of curiosity, and a few highly cited positive studies have flagged images. I built the tool myself, so I know its limits, but I'm wondering whether manipulated images could partly explain why some positive results look so clean.

**Assistant:** It's an interesting hypothesis, but be careful with how you present it. Flags from an automated detector are leads, not findings, and a small number of cases can't explain a citation-wide pattern. If you pursue it, have independent people review each flag blind to citation count and result direction. Then you could test whether confirmed image problems are more common in positive studies than null ones. Any individual case should go through proper integrity channels rather than being named in a paper.

**User:** Agreed, I won't name anything. I'll treat it as an exploratory secondary analysis with blinded confirmation. And if other people doing citation or integrity work want to use the detector, I'm glad to share it; it's been sitting on my drive doing nothing most of the time.

**Assistant:** That sounds like a responsible plan. Pre-specifying the secondary analysis and reporting it as exploratory will protect you from accusations of fishing. If you share the detector, a short usage guide explaining that flags require human review would help others use it carefully. You might also invite collaborators to contribute labeled examples, which would improve the tool's accuracy over time.
