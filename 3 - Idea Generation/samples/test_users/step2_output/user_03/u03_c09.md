**User:** Update on the ensemble calibration. A member-by-member rescaling with a lead-time-dependent inflation factor got my spread-skill ratio from about 0.5 to around 0.95 at day five, and the spatial fields stay coherent because it scales deviations from the ensemble mean. But reliability diagrams for high temperature thresholds still look overconfident. I think calibration works on average and breaks in the tails.

**Assistant:** That is a common result. Global inflation fixes the bulk of the distribution, but if the under-dispersion is worse during high-impact regimes, a single factor per lead time will not be enough. You could make the inflation factor depend on predictors such as the ensemble mean anomaly, the flow regime, or the season. Another option is to fit calibration with a tail-weighted score so the optimisation focuses on the thresholds that matter. Check also that your verification sample in the tails is large enough to be confident in the reliability diagram.

**User:** State-dependent inflation is my next step. My suspicion is that the model is most overconfident exactly when it's also most biased, during the record events, which would tie back to the extrapolation issue. If that's right, post-processing alone can't fully fix it.

**Assistant:** That is a plausible and important point. Post-processing can widen spread and correct systematic bias it has seen, but it cannot recover information the model never produces about unprecedented magnitudes. Showing that overconfidence and bias co-occur on record events would be a strong argument for combining calibration with model-side changes, such as probabilistic training objectives or hybrid physics-ML approaches. A simple scatter of per-event bias against per-event spread-skill ratio would make the case visually.
