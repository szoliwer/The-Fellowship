**User:** Three groups outside my lab want to try my GPU packing scheduler, and one of them uses a different cluster manager than we do. I'm happy to share the code and walk people through setting it up, it's saved us a huge amount of GPU time. But I'm a bit worried about becoming everyone's unpaid support desk. How do people usually handle sharing internal research tools like this?

**Assistant:** A common approach is to open-source the code with a clear license and a statement of support expectations, for example best-effort, issues tracked publicly, no guaranteed response time. Hold a single onboarding session or record a walkthrough so you are not repeating yourself. Encourage adopters to contribute their adapters, such as support for the other cluster manager, back to the repository. Some people also write a short paper or technical note, which gives citable credit and makes the time investment more defensible.

**User:** A recorded walkthrough plus a technical note sounds right. I'd actually like it to be a two-way thing, if another lab adapts it for their scheduler I'd learn something too. And frankly, sharing know-how on running lots of small experiments efficiently is something I'm good at and enjoy, more than people might expect from someone who mostly does forecasting.

**Assistant:** That is a great position to be in, and it can open collaborations, since groups running many small experiments often have interesting science problems too. You might list the tool and your willingness to advise on research-computing efficiency in your profile or lab page. A small contributors guide that explains how adapters plug in will make two-way exchange much easier.
