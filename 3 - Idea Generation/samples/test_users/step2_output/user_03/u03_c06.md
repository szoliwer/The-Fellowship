**User:** I tried ensemble model output statistics on my under-dispersed ML ensemble for two-metre temperature. CRPS improves a lot and the rank histograms are much flatter. But it only works per gridpoint and per variable, and when I look at the calibrated fields they lose spatial coherence. For things like heatwave area or wind across a region that seems like a problem. Am I missing something?

**Assistant:** No, that is a known limitation. Univariate calibration fixes marginal distributions at each location but does not preserve dependence across space, time, or variables. The standard remedy is to reorder calibrated samples using a dependence template. Ensemble copula coupling uses the rank order of the raw ensemble members to reconstruct multivariate structure, while the Schaake shuffle uses historical observations as the template. With an under-dispersed raw ensemble, ensemble copula coupling may inherit overly smooth dependence, so the Schaake shuffle or a hybrid can work better for extreme-event metrics.

**User:** I hadn't thought about the raw ensemble's dependence structure also being wrong, but that makes sense since it's all too smooth. My worry with Schaake shuffle is that historical templates might not represent the extreme events I care about. I'm starting to think calibration and the extremes question are entangled for me.

**Assistant:** They are entangled. Calibration methods fitted on typical days may not hold in the tails, and dependence templates from the past may not capture unprecedented events. You could evaluate calibration separately on the subset of extreme cases, using threshold-weighted CRPS, to see whether calibrated ensembles remain reliable in the tails. Another route is to calibrate with methods that condition on the flow regime, so that blocking situations, for example, get their own correction.
