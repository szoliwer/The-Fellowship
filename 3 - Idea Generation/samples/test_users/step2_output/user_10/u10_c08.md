**User:** My figures use red and green to distinguish influent and effluent samples, and a co-author pointed out that's bad for colourblind readers. I'm plotting in matplotlib with four groups total. What palette should I switch to, and is there a quick way to check how it looks?

**Assistant:** For four groups, the Okabe-Ito palette is a solid choice; for example orange #E69F00, sky blue #56B4E9, bluish green #009E73 and vermillion #D55E00 are distinguishable for most colour vision types. Matplotlib's tab10 is less ideal. You can also add marker shapes or line styles as a second cue. To check, simulators such as the colorspacious package in Python, or online colour blindness simulators, let you preview your figure under different deficiencies.

**User:** I'll switch to the Okabe-Ito colours and add different marker shapes as a second cue. One more question: should the same colour always mean the same group across every figure in the paper and the supplement? Right now each figure script picks colours independently, so influent is orange in one plot and blue in another, which probably confuses people.

**Assistant:** Yes, a fixed colour-to-group mapping across the whole paper helps readers a lot, and mismatched colours between figures are a common reviewer complaint. Define a small dictionary at the top of a shared plotting module that maps each group name to its colour and marker, then import it in every figure script. That makes future changes a one-line edit. As a final check, print or export a figure in greyscale; if the groups remain distinguishable through markers and lightness differences, your figures will work for nearly all readers.
