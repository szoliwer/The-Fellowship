Wastewater treatment plants are recognized hotspots for antibiotic resistance. In this study we investigated whether microplastic particles present in wastewater serve as carriers that accumulate antibiotic-resistance genes relative to the bulk water phase. Samples were collected from influent and effluent at three municipal treatment plants over two seasons, and microplastics were separated by density flotation and filtration. ARG abundance was quantified by qPCR and normalized to 16S rRNA gene copies. Across all plants, normalized ARG abundance on microplastics was consistently higher than in the corresponding water samples, and enrichment persisted in effluent. These findings suggest microplastics may facilitate the dissemination of resistance genes into receiving waters, with implications for treatment design and monitoring.
