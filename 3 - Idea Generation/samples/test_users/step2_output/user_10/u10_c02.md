**User:** I need to convert a list of concentrations from mg/L to µM for a supplementary table. The compounds are nitrate at 12.4 mg/L, phosphate at 3.1 mg/L, and ammonium at 0.85 mg/L. I always second-guess myself on which molar mass to use for the ions. Can you do the conversions and show the formula?

**Assistant:** The formula is µM = (mg/L ÷ molar mass in g/mol) × 1000. Using the ion molar masses: nitrate NO3- is 62.00 g/mol, so 12.4 ÷ 62.00 × 1000 = 200 µM. Phosphate PO4 3- is 94.97 g/mol, so 3.1 ÷ 94.97 × 1000 ≈ 32.6 µM. Ammonium NH4+ is 18.04 g/mol, so 0.85 ÷ 18.04 × 1000 ≈ 47.1 µM. Double-check whether your lab reports these as the ion or as nitrogen or phosphorus, since that changes the molar mass.

**User:** Good catch, I just checked the lab sheet and the nitrate and ammonium values are actually reported as N, and phosphate is reported as P, not as the full ions. That's what the analyzer outputs by default. Can you redo all three conversions with that in mind, and tell me how I should label the column headers so nobody misreads them later?

**Assistant:** Sure. When values are reported as N or P, divide by the atomic mass of the element rather than the ion. Nitrate-N: 12.4 ÷ 14.007 × 1000 ≈ 885 µM. Phosphate-P: 3.1 ÷ 30.974 × 1000 ≈ 100 µM. Ammonium-N: 0.85 ÷ 14.007 × 1000 ≈ 60.7 µM. For the headers, write NO3-N (µM), PO4-P (µM) and NH4-N (µM) so the basis is explicit. A short footnote giving the conversion formula and the atomic masses used will let anyone reproduce the numbers without guessing which molar mass you applied.
