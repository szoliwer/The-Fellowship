**User:** Working on robustness for the class size paper. Reviewers in education econ always ask for placebo cutoffs, so I plan to estimate fake discontinuities at enrolments of 20, 25, 40, and 45 where nothing should happen. I'm also going to show the bandwidth sensitivity plot. Anything else I'm missing? I'd like the robustness section to really address the manipulation concern, since that's what I expect people to push hardest on.

**Assistant:** Placebo cutoffs and bandwidth sensitivity are standard and good. Additional checks to consider: placebo outcomes, such as test scores from the year before the class assignment, which should not jump at the cutoff; covariate balance tables at each threshold; and estimates that use only schools with no evidence of bunching. For manipulation specifically, you could show that results are similar when using the earlier projected enrolment as the running variable, and include the bounding exercise. A heterogeneity check by budget pressure would also help connect the manipulation evidence to your main results.

**User:** Good call on lagged test scores as a placebo outcome, I hadn't planned that. Splitting by bunching is tricky though, because bunching is a school-level behaviour and those schools probably differ in other ways too. I think I'll present it as descriptive rather than causal. I also want to add a sentence on whether the cap rule has been enforced consistently over time, since enforcement got stricter after a funding change.

**Assistant:** Presenting the bunching split as descriptive is sensible; you avoid overclaiming while still showing that the results hold where manipulation looks minimal. The enforcement change is worth more than a sentence if you have the data. A stricter cap after the funding change would mean a sharper first stage in later years, and comparing estimates before and after could serve as a check on whether the effect is stable. If enforcement also reduced manipulation, the density test should show less bunching in later years, which would be a nice piece of supporting evidence.
