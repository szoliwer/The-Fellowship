**User:** Update on the class size RD. The first stage looks great at the 30 cutoff, predicted class size jumps and actual class size follows closely. But at 60 and 90 the first stage is much weaker, because larger schools apparently have more flexibility with mixed-grade classes and teaching assistants. The F-statistics there are around 6 or 7. Should I just drop the upper cutoffs, or is there a way to use them without weak-instrument problems?

**Assistant:** Weak first stages at the upper cutoffs are common in this kind of design. You have several options. First, report the estimates for the first cutoff as the main result and present the others as secondary, using weak-instrument-robust inference such as confidence intervals based on inverting a weak-IV-robust test. Second, if you pool cutoffs, the strong first stage at 30 can carry the joint estimate, but then the estimate mostly reflects the first cutoff. Third, think about whether the weaker compliance at higher cutoffs is itself informative about how schools respond to the rule.

**User:** I like the weak-IV-robust confidence interval approach. My bigger concern is interpretation: at 30 the compliers are mostly small rural schools, and those are different from the urban schools around 60 and 90. So even if I pool, the LATE is really about small schools. I want to be upfront that this design can't say much about class size reductions in large urban schools, which is unfortunately where the policy debate is.

**Assistant:** That is a careful point and worth stating clearly in the paper. RD estimates are local both to the cutoff and to the compliers at that cutoff, so external validity is limited. You could describe the complier characteristics at each threshold to make this concrete, comparing school size, urbanicity, and student demographics. If you want to say something about larger schools, you might pair the RD with another source of variation, such as a policy change in caps, or use the covariates to reweight the estimates toward the urban population, while being clear about the extra assumptions.
