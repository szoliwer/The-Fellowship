**User:** I'm trying to combine my own bone marrow single-cell data with two public datasets from other labs. Before correction the cells cluster almost perfectly by dataset, which is obviously technical. I ran Harmony and it looks mixed now, but I'm worried I've over-corrected and erased real biological differences, since one public dataset is from older mice. How do I tell good integration from over-integration?

**Assistant:** That's the central tension in integration. Use metrics on both sides: batch mixing scores like kBET or iLISI tell you whether batches mix, while biological conservation metrics like cell-type ARI or cLISI tell you whether known cell types stay separated. Benchmarking frameworks report both together. For your age concern, a useful check is to look for differential expression of known age-associated genes within matched cell types after integration; if those signals vanish, you've likely over-corrected. Note that Harmony corrects the embedding, not the counts, so DE on raw counts with batch as a covariate is still possible.

**User:** That's helpful, I didn't realise Harmony only touches the embedding. I think I'll compare Harmony with scVI too, since scVI lets me include age as a separate covariate. Do you think it's legitimate to include age as a biological covariate while correcting for lab, given that age and lab are partly confounded in my design?

**Assistant:** It's legitimate only to the extent the design allows it. If every old-mouse sample comes from one lab, lab and age are perfectly confounded and no method can separate them; the model will attribute the difference to whichever variable you tell it is technical. If there's partial overlap, for example your own data includes some older animals, scVI can use that overlap to estimate both effects. I'd make that limitation explicit and check results on the overlapping samples alone as a sanity check.
