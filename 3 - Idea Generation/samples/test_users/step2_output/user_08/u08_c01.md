**User:** I have a 10x dataset of about 40k cells from mouse bone marrow, sorted for stem and progenitor populations. I want to order the cells along pseudotime from the most primitive stem cells toward erythroid and myeloid states. I tried diffusion pseudotime with a root cell I picked by hand from the stem cluster, but I'm nervous the ordering is very sensitive to which root I pick. How do people usually handle that?

**Assistant:** Root sensitivity is a common concern. A few practical steps: choose the root using a marker-based score rather than a single hand-picked cell, for example the cell with the highest stemness signature, or the extreme of the first diffusion component within the stem cluster. Then rerun with several candidate roots from that cluster and check the rank correlation of the resulting orderings. If correlations are high, say above 0.9, your ordering is robust. Also check the number of neighbours and diffusion components, since those affect pseudotime more than people expect.

**User:** Good idea, I'll do the multiple-roots check. Another thing: my erythroid cells look nicely ordered but the myeloid side is a mess, with neutrophil and monocyte precursors mixed together in pseudotime. I suspect a single linear ordering just doesn't make sense once you pass the point where they diverge. Should I be computing the ordering separately per lineage?

**Assistant:** Your suspicion is probably right. A single pseudotime value can't represent divergent paths; cells at the same pseudotime on different branches look 'mixed' in a one-dimensional view. Methods like Slingshot or Palantir fit separate curves or compute branch probabilities, giving each cell a pseudotime per lineage plus a weight for how committed it is. Alternatively, subset to each myeloid branch after identifying the split and order those cells separately. Checking known markers along each branch, like Elane for neutrophils and Irf8 for monocytes, will help confirm the ordering is biologically sensible.
