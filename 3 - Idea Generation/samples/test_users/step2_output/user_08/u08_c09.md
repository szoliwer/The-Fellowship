**User:** Silly question while procrastinating. Could you take lineage or trajectory inference methods from biology and apply them to startups? Like treat each company as a cell, with features such as headcount, funding stage and product category, and infer how companies 'differentiate' into, say, acquired, IPO or dead. I don't actually plan to do this, I'm just wondering whether the analogy holds up at all.

**Assistant:** It's a fun analogy and partly works. You could embed companies by feature vectors over time, build a neighbourhood graph and look for branching structure leading to terminal states like acquisition or shutdown. But there are differences: you actually have real time-series per company, so you don't need pseudotime to reconstruct order from snapshots, which is the main point of those methods in biology. Standard survival analysis or sequence clustering might be more appropriate. The branching-fate framing could still be a neat visualisation.

**User:** Ha, fair point, the whole reason we need those tricks is that we destroy the cell when we measure it. With startups you can just follow them over time. I guess the analogy is more like lineage tracing than snapshot inference. Anyway, enough procrastinating, but thanks for humouring me.

**Assistant:** Exactly, startups come with their own longitudinal data, closer to live imaging than to a destructive snapshot. Where it could get interesting is founders and employees moving between companies, which creates something like a genealogy of firms, similar to how spinoffs inherit traits from parents. That's been studied in economics as corporate genealogy. But yes, it's more of a thought experiment than a project. Good luck getting back to the real work.
