**User:** I ran the stack pressure scan. Interfacial resistance drops steadily up to about 8 MPa, and cells last longer. Above that, at 15 MPa, two of four cells shorted early. Post-mortem shows lithium in some cracks. So it seems like there's a sweet spot, but I'm still not sure if the improved cycling at moderate pressure is real interface improvement or just masking void formation. How could I tell?

**Assistant:** One way is to measure void formation directly. You could cycle at different pressures and then image the interface for voids, or use impedance during stripping to track contact loss in real time. If moderate pressure maintains contact without lithium intrusion, then it's improving the interface. If voids still form but are refilled by creep, that's masking, and you'd expect failure once pressure is reduced or cycling rate increases.

**User:** Reducing the pressure mid test is a good idea. If cells cycled at 8 MPa suddenly degrade when dropped to 2 MPa, that would mean the pressure was compensating rather than fixing anything. I think that's the more honest test of whether the interface is good.

**Assistant:** Exactly, a pressure step down test reveals whether the interface is intrinsically stable. You could also compare with cells that have an interlayer at the same pressures; if interlayer cells tolerate pressure reduction better, that indicates a genuine interface improvement rather than mechanical compensation. Tracking impedance continuously through the step down would show how quickly contact is lost, which is useful for estimating how a real pack would behave.
