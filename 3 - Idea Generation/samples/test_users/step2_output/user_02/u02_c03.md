**User:** I'm trying to standardise how we measure critical current density in our Li/LLZO/Li symmetric cells. Right now we step up current every few cycles until shorting, but results vary a lot between nominally identical pellets. I think part of it is interfacial contact and part is random defects at grain boundaries. How do people get reproducible CCD numbers?

**Assistant:** CCD is notoriously sensitive to protocol. Key factors include the current step size and dwell time, areal capacity per step, temperature, stack pressure, and interface preparation. Reproducibility improves if you remove surface carbonates before assembly, control lithium thickness, and report full voltage profiles rather than only the shorting current. Running more cells per condition and reporting distributions, rather than single values, helps account for defect-driven variability.

**User:** Reporting distributions makes sense. I suspect the spread is real and tells us something, maybe the weakest grain boundary path determines failure. If that's true, CCD would follow something like weakest-link statistics. I could test that by varying pellet thickness and area and seeing if failure probability scales the way it should.

**Assistant:** That's a nice idea. If failure is controlled by the most vulnerable defect or grain boundary path, then larger electrode areas should fail at lower current on average, and you could fit the distribution to a Weibull model. Thickness dependence would tell you whether penetration needs a full connected path or whether nucleation is the limiting step. That would be a neat way to link microstructure statistics to failure.
