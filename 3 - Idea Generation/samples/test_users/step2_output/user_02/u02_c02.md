**User:** All our dendrite evidence comes from post-mortem cross sections, which is frustrating because we only see the end state and sample prep might create artefacts. I'd really like to watch lithium penetrate the electrolyte while the cell is cycling. I've heard of synchrotron X-ray tomography for this, but I've never used a beamline and don't know how to get access. What are the options?

**Assistant:** Operando imaging options include synchrotron X-ray tomography, which can resolve lithium filaments and cracks in 3D over time; lab-based X-ray CT, which has lower resolution and slower acquisition; operando optical microscopy using a side-view cell geometry; and in-situ electron microscopy for very small cells. Synchrotron access usually goes through competitive proposals at user facilities, and success often depends on having an experienced beamline user or staff scientist involved who knows cell design and data reconstruction.

**User:** That's what I was afraid of. Our group has no synchrotron experience and I don't think we'd write a competitive proposal on our own. We are good at making the electrolyte pellets and cycling them, so I'd love to partner with someone who already does operando tomography of batteries. They'd bring the imaging expertise, we'd bring well characterised samples.

**Assistant:** That kind of partnership is common and works well. Imaging groups often look for interesting, well-characterised samples, and your ability to make controlled microstructures, for instance different grain sizes, is valuable to them. You'd also need to co-design an operando cell that fits the beamline, maintains stack pressure and allows X-ray transmission through a thin electrolyte. Preparing a short summary of your samples, failure behaviour and key questions would make it easier to approach potential collaborators.
