**User:** While we wait to figure out synchrotron access, I'm wondering whether we could do some operando optical microscopy ourselves. A side-view cell where we look at the edge of the LLZO pellet while cycling. I'm not sure the resolution would be enough to see dendrites starting at grain boundaries, and I have no experience building these cells.

**Assistant:** Optical microscopy can capture lithium growth at the edge of a cell at micron resolution, which is sometimes enough to see filaments emerging and propagating, though not the earliest nucleation inside grain boundaries. The main challenges are sealing the cell to keep it inert, applying stack pressure while keeping the edge visible, and making sure the edge behaves like the bulk. Polishing the edge can introduce surface defects that become preferential paths.

**User:** Right, so the edge might be a special case. I think optical is useful as a stopgap but what we really need is 3D imaging inside the pellet while it's cycling. I'd rather find a group that has already built operando cells for tomography or electron microscopy than spend a year reinventing it badly.

**Assistant:** That seems like a sensible prioritisation. Groups with established operando cells have already solved sealing, pressure and alignment problems, and they know the imaging artefacts. You could still use a simple optical cell for quick screening of conditions, then bring the most interesting samples to a collaborator for 3D operando imaging. Having screening data ready also makes beamtime more efficient, because you can arrive knowing which conditions produce the most informative failures.
