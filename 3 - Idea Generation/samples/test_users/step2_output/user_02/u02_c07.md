**User:** Many papers add thin interlayers like gold or aluminium oxide between lithium and LLZO to improve wetting. They do lower interfacial resistance in our hands, but I'm not convinced they help with grain boundary penetration, since dendrites seem to nucleate inside the electrolyte too. Am I right that interlayers only address the interface part of the problem?

**Assistant:** Largely yes. Interlayers mostly improve wetting and current distribution at the interface, reducing hot spots that initiate dendrites from the anode side. They don't change the electronic properties or mechanics of grain boundaries deep in the electrolyte, so they won't stop lithium nucleating internally if that's a main mechanism. That said, by homogenising current, they can delay failure even if the internal mechanism remains.

**User:** That's my thinking too. I want to design an experiment that separates interface-initiated dendrites from internally nucleated ones. Maybe cells with and without interlayers, cycled to just before shorting, then sectioned to see where lithium deposits are located. If internal deposits are present regardless of interlayer, that's telling.

**Assistant:** That design is good. Sectioning at several stages before shorting would reveal the sequence of events. Pair it with careful sample prep, such as cryo or ion milling under inert conditions, to avoid smearing lithium. Quantifying isolated lithium deposits inside the electrolyte, not connected to either electrode, would be strong evidence for internal nucleation. Mapping how the number of such deposits changes with cycling stage would also tell you whether internal growth precedes or follows interface penetration.
