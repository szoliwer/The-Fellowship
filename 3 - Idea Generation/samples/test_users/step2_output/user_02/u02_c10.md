**User:** I'm drafting a short note to approach groups who do operando X-ray tomography or operando electron microscopy on batteries. What we offer: controlled LLZO microstructures with different grain sizes and doping, statistics on failure, and post-mortem cross sections. What we need: the ability to actually see lithium enter grain boundaries during cycling. How should I frame it so it's appealing to them?

**Assistant:** Lead with the scientific question, whether lithium nucleates inside grain boundaries or propagates from the interface, and why operando imaging is uniquely able to answer it. Then highlight what you bring: well-characterised samples with systematically varied microstructure, a statistical dataset of failure behaviour, and complementary post-mortem analysis. Suggest a concrete first experiment, such as imaging two grain sizes under identical conditions, and note practical matters like cell compatibility and sample dimensions.

**User:** I like starting with the question. I'll also say that we can make samples thin enough for transmission and that we're happy to adapt our pellet geometry to their cell. I really don't want to try to learn tomography reconstruction from scratch, so the collaboration has to be genuinely two-sided.

**Assistant:** That's the right attitude, and offering to adapt sample geometry signals that you've thought about their constraints. Clarify roles and shared outputs early, such as co-authorship and data sharing. A two-sided collaboration where you supply samples and failure statistics and they provide imaging expertise is attractive because each side contributes something the other can't easily do. Attaching one or two representative post-mortem images and a failure statistics plot to the note would make it concrete and memorable.
