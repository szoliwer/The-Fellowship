**User:** We got some interesting input on our grain boundary hypothesis. Dr. Elena Varga at Northfield Institute shared unpublished atom probe data showing aluminium and lanthanum segregation at grain boundaries in samples similar to ours. That would support the idea that boundaries have different local chemistry and maybe higher electronic conductivity. How should I think about connecting segregation to dendrite growth in my cycling experiments?

**Assistant:** Segregation data is a useful link. If dopants or secondary phases at boundaries lower the band gap or introduce electronic states, lithium ions could be reduced within the boundary before reaching the anode. To connect this to your cycling, you could vary the dopant level or sintering conditions to change segregation, then measure electronic conductivity and critical current density. If failure correlates with segregation strength, that supports a chemical mechanism rather than purely mechanical cracking.

**User:** We actually have three batches with different aluminium doping levels. I could cycle cells from each batch and do cross sections afterwards to see whether the lithium paths follow boundaries more in the high aluminium samples. I'd need to be careful though, since doping also changes grain size.

**Assistant:** That's a good experiment, and you're right about the confound. You could try to match grain size across batches by adjusting sintering time, or measure grain size and include it as a covariate. Quantifying the fraction of lithium paths that are intergranular versus transgranular in each batch would give you a clear metric to compare. Measuring electronic conductivity on each batch with blocking electrodes would close the loop between composition, transport and failure.
